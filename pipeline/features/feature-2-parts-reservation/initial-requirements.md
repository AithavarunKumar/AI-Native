# Feature 2 — Parts Reservation

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Requirements — initial draft for review |
| **One-line intent** | Reserve the required parts in inventory when a technician job is scheduled so unavailable or insufficient stock is handled consistently. |
| **Depends on** | Feature 1's committed job slot, including its overlap-safe scheduling outcome and the `TRAVEL_BUFFER_MINUTES = 45` rule in `pipeline/decisions/feature-1-decisions.md`. |

## 1. Feature overview

After Feature 1 confirms and creates a technician job for a valid, conflict-free slot, Parts Reservation determines whether the job's required parts are available. Parts are reserved against the job so they cannot be allocated to another job until the reservation is used or released.

This feature is part of the job scheduling flow but does not replace Feature 1 scheduling. A parts-reservation failure may prevent final job confirmation, but it must not silently alter or cancel the Feature 1 technician booking. The reservation and its associated job must be traceable by job ID.

## 2. User stories

- **US-1:** As a dispatcher, I want to schedule a job and have required parts checked atomically, so that I know whether the job can be supported by current inventory.
- **US-2:** As a dispatcher, I want to see exactly which parts and quantities are missing or partially unavailable, so that I can choose a valid resolution before confirming the job.
- **US-3:** As a service manager, I want a successful reservation to remain linked to the job, so that inventory is not reused by another job.
- **US-4:** As a dispatcher, I want cancelling a job to release its reserved parts, so that inventory becomes available for future work.
- **US-5:** As a dispatcher, I want repeated reservation requests for the same job to be idempotent, so that retries do not double-reserve stock.
- **US-6:** As a service manager, I want to distinguish unavailable stock from an optional backorder, so that automatic purchasing is not implied by reservation status.

## 3. Testable acceptance criteria

- **AC-1 — Inventory check:** When a scheduled job includes a required part quantity, the system checks current unreserved/available inventory and reserves only the requested quantity not already allocated elsewhere.
- **AC-2 — Full availability:** When every required part has sufficient stock, the system creates one reservation per part for the job and reports success with the job ID and reserved quantities.
- **AC-3 — Partial stock:** When any required part has insufficient stock, no quantity for that part is reserved if the feature follows an all-or-nothing policy; [OPEN QUESTION] whether partial reservations are allowed. The initial assumption is all-or-nothing: inventory is unchanged until the whole bill of parts can be reserved.
- **AC-4 — Unavailable stock:** When a required part has zero or otherwise unavailable stock, the system rejects reservation and returns the part ID, requested quantity, available quantity, and an unavailable status.
- **AC-5 — Atomicity:** A failed reservation leaves inventory and reservations unchanged for all required parts; no partial allocation remains.
- **AC-6 — Duplicate reservation:** Repeating a reservation request with the same job ID and same part requirements returns the existing reservation unchanged and does not increment or re-decrement stock.
- **AC-7 — Conflicting retry:** Repeating a reservation request for the same job ID with changed requirements is rejected rather than modifying or replacing the existing reservation.
- **AC-8 — Unknown job:** A reservation request for a job that does not exist or was not successfully scheduled is rejected.
- **AC-9 — Unknown/zero quantity parts:** A required part that does not exist in inventory is treated as unavailable; a quantity of zero or less is rejected as invalid.
- **AC-10 — Cancellation:** When a job is cancelled, all active reservations for that job are released exactly once and the inventory becomes available again.
- **AC-11 — Duplicate cancellation:** Releasing an already-released reservation is idempotent and does not add stock twice.
- **AC-12 — Backorder:** Backorders are not created automatically by this feature. If business policy permits a later manual or future automated backorder flow, the reservation response identifies the unmet quantity as backorder-eligible only with an explicit [OPEN QUESTION] policy decision.
- **AC-13 — Feature 1 dependency:** A reservation operation is performed only for a job whose technician slot has been successfully committed by Feature 1. It does not check or recreate technician overlap rules and does not reserve inventory for a rejected technician slot.
- **AC-14 — Traceability:** Every reservation records the job ID and each part ID/quantity reserved, and reservation status is queryable by job ID.

## 4. Edge cases

- **Partial stock:** Only some requested quantities are available. The initial policy is all-parts atomic reservation; the user review must confirm whether partial stock should instead produce a partial reservation plus a backorder flag.
- **Unavailable stock:** One or more parts have zero or no inventory record. The job's reservation response must be explicit and not report a generic success.
- **Duplicate reservations:** The same job and requirements retried concurrently or sequentially must share the same reservation and not consume stock twice.
- **Reservation conflicts:** The same job requests materially different parts or quantities after a reservation exists; reject the changed request.
- **Concurrent jobs:** Two jobs reserve the last unit of a part at the same time. Exactly one succeeds; the loser receives insufficient-stock status.
- **Cancellation or job completion:** Cancellation releases active reservation. Completion consumes the reserved parts, with a separate status transition that can be implemented only if that domain behavior is included in scope; otherwise the reservation remains active until a later explicit release/consume action. [OPEN QUESTION]
- **Backorders:** No automatic purchase or backorder is assumed. Any unmet quantity may be identified for manual follow-up, but that follow-up is outside this feature.
- **Invalid part list:** A job with no required parts is valid only if its work type legitimately requires no parts; otherwise it is rejected or flagged for review. [OPEN QUESTION]
- **Time validity:** Scheduling time changes after reservation are out of scope unless the same job lifecycle explicitly defines re-reservation; the initial assumption is reservations do not depend on technician travel time.
- **Parts already in use:** Inventory may be allocated to active reservations for other jobs; only net available quantity may be reserved.

## 5. Dependencies on Feature 1

- Feature 2 depends specifically on the Feature 1 decision that a job has a committed, non-overlapping technician slot when it is returned as successfully scheduled.
- The Feature 1 overlap formula and `TRAVEL_BUFFER_MINUTES = 45` remain authoritative. Parts reservation must not duplicate or weaken technician availability validation.
- A job must be linked to the Feature 1 job ID and technician ID; reservation does not create or update a technician booking.
- Feature 1 HTTP behavior remains in effect: an overlap conflict is `409`, technician not found is `404`, and invalid request fields are `400`. Parts reservation adds inventory outcomes only after a job exists, [OPEN QUESTION] whether the combined schedule-and-reserve operation is synchronous or separate.
- If scheduling and reservation are a single user action, the system must define whether a job remains scheduled with a parts failure or whether the complete operation rolls back to cancel the technician booking. Initial requirement assumption: preserve the committed Feature 1 slot and report the parts failure separately.

## 6. Success metrics

- 100% of successful reservations correspond to an existing scheduled job.
- 100% of successful reservations consume no more than net available inventory.
- 0 duplicate quantity increases for identical/retried reservation requests.
- 100% of failed all-or-nothing reservations leave stock and reservation records unchanged.
- 100% of cancelled jobs release active reservations exactly once.
- 100% of insufficient/unavailable responses include the affected part ID and requested versus available quantity.
- The same-stock concurrency test yields one success and no oversubscription for one remaining unit.

## 7. Assumptions and open questions

### Assumptions

- Inventory is mock/in-memory for this feature and no external inventory system or database is assumed.
- Required parts are supplied with a required quantity by job type or job request; the initial model assumes each part appears once in a job's requirements.
- Available quantity means physical quantity less all currently active reservations.
- Initial reservation failure policy is all-or-nothing; nothing is reserved when a single required part is insufficient.
- Repeated identical reservations are idempotent.
- Cancellation releases reservations; stock consumption on completion is [OPEN QUESTION] and not currently assumed.
- Feature 1 remains the source of truth for technician availability and has already committed a booked slot before inventory work begins.

### Open questions for review

1. Should a scheduled job proceed with only partial stock, or must every required part be fully reserved before success?
2. If parts reservation fails, does the Feature 1 technician slot remain booked, or should a single booking action roll it back?
3. Is inventory reservation a synchronous part of `POST /api/jobs`, or a separate API operation after a job exists?
4. When are reserved parts consumed: on job start, at completion, or by an explicit parts-use action?
5. Should cancellation, rescheduling, or technician reassignment change or move reservations automatically?
6. Are backorders represented as data/status records, or only surfaced as unmet quantities for manual follow-up?
7. Are zero-part jobs allowed, and how are required parts are defined for a job type?
8. What cancellation API/status transition is available in Feature 1 or must this feature introduce?
9. What HTTP status and response shape should represent partial/unavailable inventory outcomes: `409 Conflict`, `422 Unprocessable Entity`, or another convention?
